# Start Here — Applied Data Analysis Track

Welcome! This document is your home base for the next 16 weeks. Bookmark it. Come back to it whenever you're unsure where something lives or what to do next.

---

## What you're going to learn

Over 16 one-hour sessions, you'll learn to:

1. Work with Python, specifically for data analysis
2. Download and work with real health data from NHANES (CDC's national health survey)
3. Clean, merge, and analyze that data using Pandas
4. Create research-grade charts
5. Complete a mini-research project on a question *you* choose

By the end, you'll have a portfolio of work and — more importantly — the skill to do this again on your own.

---

## How this course works

Each week has a rhythm. Your time investment is roughly **1 hour of class + 3–4 hours of work on your own**. That ratio is deliberate. The class is where we go deep and unstick you. The out-of-class time is where the actual learning sticks.

Here's the weekly loop:

### 1. Pre-read (2 days before class, ~30–45 min)

Before every session (except Session 1), you'll have a short pre-read — usually a Colab notebook with some reading and a few tiny exercises. **Do this before class.** If you skip it, the class doesn't work — the instructor will be teaching the wrong level.

Each pre-read ends with 3 self-check questions. They're not graded. They're your signal: if you can answer them, you're ready for class. If you can't, tell the instructor before class so we adjust.

### 2. The live session (1 hour)

Here's what happens in class:

- **First 10 min:** You walk through your homework. What did you do? Where did you get stuck?
- **Next 10 min:** Instructor introduces today's new concept with a live example.
- **Next 20 min:** You drive. Your fingers on the keyboard. Instructor asks questions, guides.
- **Next 15 min:** We deliberately hit a hard problem — the kind you'll face in real analysis.
- **Last 5 min:** Wrap up. Homework for the week. Pre-read for next week.

### 3. Consolidation (right after class, 15 min)

The single most valuable 15 minutes of your week:

- Save the session notebook with a clear name
- Write 2–3 lines in your **learning journal**: what clicked, what's still fuzzy
- Glance at the artifact you made today

Don't skip this. Research shows review-right-after doubles retention. It's boring. Do it anyway.

### 4. Homework (across the week, 2–3 hours)

Homework is designed so you'll get stuck *once*, productively. That's the point. If homework feels breezy, tell me — we're underpacing. If you can't start, ping me on WhatsApp — we'll unblock you.

Every homework produces one artifact (a chart, a cleaned file, a short analysis). These accumulate. By Session 16, you'll have a folder of 16 artifacts showing your arc.

## What's out of scope (so you don't go down rabbit holes)

These are not part of this track. If you're curious, note them in your journal and we'll discuss later:

- Machine learning
- Statistical inference (t-tests, regression, p-values)
- Survey-weighted analysis (NHANES has a specific sampling design — we're acknowledging this and setting it aside)
- R / RStudio
- Git / GitHub
- Building web apps or dashboards

We're going **deep** on one workflow (Python → Pandas → NHANES → research output), not broad across many tools. That depth is the point.

## If you get stuck

Between classes, in order of what to try first:

1. Look at your **reference sheet** — you've probably seen this before
2. Look at the **troubleshooting guide** — common issues and fixes
3. Re-read the relevant **pre-read**
4. Ping the **WhatsApp group** with a screenshot and what you tried
5. Save it for class — put it on the top of your homework review

Rule of thumb: don't stare at an error for more than 15 minutes. Ping us. That's what we're here for.
